My research explores how collective light–matter interactions generate nonclassical optical states, critical behavior, and useful quantum resources in open many-body systems.

<div class="card-grid">
  <div class="info-card">
    <h3>Squeezing and nonclassical light</h3>
    <p>I study robust squeezing in the thermodynamic limit of the two-photon Tavis–Cummings model, including photon emission rates, multi-time field correlations, Wigner functions, and photon statistics.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="info-card">
    <h3>Nonlocal quantum phase transitions</h3>
    <p>I investigate critical phase transitions driven by nonlocal quantum fluctuations using the Lipkin–Meshkov–Glick model and two-photon driven Kerr resonators.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="info-card">
    <h3>Collective nonlinear quantum optics</h3>
    <p>My PhD thesis focuses on two-photon interacting atomic ensembles, driven-dissipative phase transitions, photon blockade, and collective enhancement.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="info-card">
    <h3>Pattern formation</h3>
    <p>I model coherent drive modulation in Rydberg exciton-polariton condensates using Gross–Pitaevskii equations, hydrodynamic formulations, Floquet analysis, and Lyapunov exponents.</p>
  </div>
</div>
